How challenges build me?

View Year-In-Review

I am an academically motivated, ambitious rising Aerospace Engineer at the University of Cincinnati. I love competition and challenges. I have faced many challenges throughout my life, including moving to 3 different cities in the past 7 years, long commutes, and losing recognition for my academic merit. However, these challenges fueled my drive to succeed; I earned a Summa Cum Laude high school diploma, placing in the top 5% of the graduating class of 2026 at William Mason High School, with 70 college credits.

In India, I was identified as academically gifted and was allowed to move up a grade. In 2019, my family had to move to Canada due to my dad's work. But due to their age restrictions, I had to stay back in 5th grade for 2 months despite finishing 6th grade in India. Although initially demotivated, the additional time gave me a chance to develop new technical skills & hobbies: I got involved with programming, robotics, and chess (recently tied for first in the U1600 section in the 2026 US Chess Federation's K12 High School Nationals in Chicago). Similar to chess, I have won awards from my robotics program and coding classes. But just as I started 8th grade, we had to move to the US for another work transfer for my dad.

In the US, middle school went well. Later in 2022, I was selected for a magnet high school, which allowed me the opportunity to take college courses throughout my high school years. However, every day, I had to take a school bus journey that lasted 2 hours in the morning and evening. Spending 4 hours commuting exhausted me, but taught me excellent time management skills. I ranked no.1 & no.4 in my school in my freshman and sophomore years, respectively.

My final move was to Mason, Ohio (in 2024) due to another transfer. Entering Junior year with a 4.7 weighted GPA, I was astonished when my new school did not issue me credit for all 11 honors courses, dropping my GPA to a 4.3. Classes felt significantly more difficult as I fought the demotivation, but I made it a goal to get my GPA back. I took more college courses, worked extremely hard, and brought my GPA to a 4.5.

I was awarded the Ohio Governor's Merit Scholarship, which guaranteed me a spot in all public institutions in Ohio along with a yearly grant of $5000. However, as I converted to an F1 student to participate in the COOP program, I was notified 2 days before my tuition was due that I would no longer be eligible for the Governor's Merit Scholarship. I am very thankful to my family for taking care of me and being very supportive. This lack of scholarship has fueled me to significantly expand my horizons and work harder.
